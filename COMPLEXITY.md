# Complexity audit

DESIGN.md Part G, "Six standing rules", requires every algorithm to be of low complexity:
O(n log n) or better, O(n²) only with a recorded reason, nothing cubic or worse outside a
polynomial procedure with no known algorithm of lower degree, run only on the connected part
of its input a question mentions, and nothing cut short by a budget, a threshold or a count
of steps. This file lists every place an audit of
2026-10-30 found that breaks the rule, and the state of each. An item is closed when its
fix is in and a scaling test (size n against 8n, ratio at most 24 in instructions and, since
SCALE-bytes, in bytes allocated) passes, or when the reason it stays is recorded here.

Status: `open`, `fixed` (with the test that checks it), `kept` (with the reason).

## Complexity tradeoffs

Sites that give up some inputs, or some precision, to avoid the cost of a complete algorithm,
and that had no row before (docs/reviews/tradeoffs.md, finding 6). Each site carries a
comment beginning `Complexity tradeoff:` that names this row. Columns: where, what is given
up and the answer those inputs get, why (the cost of the complete algorithm), and the input
that triggers it with the test. Rows marked "violation" switch behavior at a size or a
magnitude and break the no-thresholds rule; they are to be replaced (ledger items TD-ends,
TD-values, CAP-power, CAP-bits), and the item that replaces one updates its row.
